Work in Progress

  1. “Public Procurement through Framework Agreements” (Dissertation project)

    Single-authored

    Public agencies repeatedly buy the same goods (vehicles, medical supplies, office equipment), and running a separate tender for every purchase is slow and costly. Framework Agreements avoid this by building a standing marketplace: a procurement agency first runs an auction to select which suppliers and products enter the menu, and public buyers such as hospitals, ministries, and universities then purchase directly from it. Setting the admission threshold involves a trade-off. Looser criteria give buyers a richer menu, but they also weaken the threat of exclusion that pushes firms to bid aggressively, so a broader menu need not make buyers better off. I study this trade-off in automobile procurement by ChileCompra, Chile's public procurement agency, which held several vehicle Framework Agreements over my sample period. I first show that products admitted at the margin of the selection cutoff capture positive market shares, so variety matters. I then develop a two-stage model of the admission auction and the purchases that follow to quantify how alternative selection rules affect prices, variety, and buyer welfare.
