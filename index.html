Welcome! I am a PhD candidate in Economics at Yale University. I work in industrial organization and public economics. My research studies competition in public procurement, and in particular how governments design the marketplaces they buy from.

In my dissertation, I study Framework Agreements, in which a procurement agency runs an auction to decide which suppliers and products public buyers can purchase from. How selective that auction is trades off competition against variety: a stricter rule makes sellers compete harder for a place, while a looser one gives buyers more products to choose from. I study this trade-off using automobile procurement by ChileCompra, Chile's public procurement agency. More on my research.

Before coming to Yale, I earned my B.A. and M.A. in Economics at the Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile, graduating first in my class in both.
